# Glossary

Shared definitions for terms used across Open Digital Policies. Definitions here take precedence over in-document glossaries where there is a conflict — individual policy models may extend but should not contradict these definitions.

This glossary is a living document. To propose an addition or correction, open an Issue with the label `glossary`.

---

## A

**Additionality** (in renewable energy)
The principle that a renewable energy claim is only environmentally meaningful if the energy purchased corresponds to generation that would not have occurred without that purchase. Buying renewable energy certificates (RECs) from existing solar or wind farms does not demonstrate additionality — those farms would have operated regardless. Additionality requires demonstrating that a specific purchase financed new generation capacity. Relevant to: Data Centers, AI Adoption.

**Algorithmic Impact Assessment (AIA)**
A structured process for evaluating the potential harms and risks of an automated decision-making system before it is deployed. Canada's federal Directive on Automated Decision-Making (2019) mandates AIAs for federal government automated systems, using a scored questionnaire that produces an impact level (I–IV) with proportionate mitigation requirements. Relevant to: Algorithmic Accountability, AI Adoption.

**Automated Decision System (ADS)**
A computational process — including one derived from machine learning — that makes or substantially assists in making decisions that affect the rights, interests, or well-being of individuals. The term is used in Canadian federal policy; equivalent terms include Automated Employment Decision Tool (NYC), algorithmic system, or AI system. Relevant to: Algorithmic Accountability, AI Adoption.

## B

**Bias Audit**
An independent, third-party assessment of an automated decision system for evidence of disparate impact across protected groups (race, sex, age, disability, etc.). NYC Local Law 144 (2023) mandates annual bias audits for automated employment decision tools. A bias audit tests outcomes but does not necessarily examine the internal logic of the system. Relevant to: Algorithmic Accountability.

---

## C

**Community Benefit Agreement (CBA)**
A legally binding contract between a developer and community representatives that ensures local residents receive specific, measurable benefits from a development project — such as local hiring commitments, infrastructure investment, or noise and pollution limits. In the data center context, CBAs are proposed as a mechanism for ensuring that data center operators contribute to the communities that host their infrastructure. Relevant to: Data Centers.

**Contribution-back**
The practice of submitting modifications made to open source software back to the upstream project under the same or a compatible licence, so that the wider community benefits. Also called "upstream contribution." Relevant to: Open Source in Government.

**Cyber Resilience Act (CRA)**
EU Regulation 2024/2847, which establishes cybersecurity requirements for products with digital elements (hardware and software). The CRA's treatment of open source software and its liability framework for public sector open source releases creates compliance uncertainty that may inadvertently discourage government open source publication. Relevant to: Open Source in Government, Digital Sovereignty.

---

## D

**Data Center**
A facility that houses computing infrastructure — servers, networking equipment, and storage — and provides the physical backbone for cloud computing, internet services, and AI model training and inference. Data centers are significant consumers of electricity and water, and their environmental footprint has grown with the expansion of AI workloads. Relevant to: Data Centers, AI Adoption.

**Data Minimisation**
The principle that only the minimum data necessary for a specified purpose should be collected, processed, and retained. A core principle of GDPR (Article 5(1)(c)) and a key standard in smart city and children's technology policy. Relevant to: Smart Cities & Privacy, Children & Technology, Algorithmic Accountability.

**Device Neutrality**
The principle that software and services should work equivalently across device brands and models, and that manufacturers should not use software to discriminate against third-party repair, parts, or services. Related to interoperability. Relevant to: Right to Repair.

**Digital Public Good (DPG)**
Open source software, open data, open AI models, open standards, or open content that adheres to privacy and other applicable laws and best practices, does no harm, and helps attain the UN Sustainable Development Goals. Defined and registered by the Digital Public Goods Alliance (DPGA). Relevant to: Open Source in Government, Digital Public Infrastructure.

**Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI)**
Shared, interoperable digital systems that function as foundational infrastructure for a society — analogous to roads, water systems, or the postal service. DPI typically includes identity systems, payment rails, and data exchange platforms. Unlike proprietary digital platforms, DPI is designed to be open, governed in the public interest, and accessible to all. Relevant to: Digital Public Infrastructure, Digital Sovereignty.

**Digital Sovereignty**
The capacity of individuals, communities, and governments to control the digital systems they depend on: to understand how they work, audit their operation, change providers, and exit without prohibitive cost. Digital sovereignty is not isolationism — it is about ensuring that dependency relationships are transparent, voluntary, and reversible. Relevant to: Digital Sovereignty, Open Source in Government.

**Disparate Impact**
A legal doctrine, originating in US employment discrimination law, that holds that a facially neutral policy or practice can constitute illegal discrimination if it disproportionately disadvantages a protected group, regardless of intent. In algorithmic contexts, disparate impact analysis examines whether an automated system produces different outcomes for different demographic groups. Relevant to: Algorithmic Accountability.

## E

**E-Waste**
Electronic waste: discarded electronic devices and components. E-waste contains toxic materials (lead, cadmium, mercury) and valuable recoverable materials. Most e-waste generated in high-income countries is informally processed in lower-income countries, creating severe environmental and health impacts for frontline communities. Relevant to: Right to Repair, Data Centers.

**Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR)**
A policy approach that holds manufacturers financially and/or operationally responsible for the end-of-life management of their products. In electronics, EPR typically requires manufacturers to fund take-back, recycling, and safe disposal programmes. Relevant to: Right to Repair.

---

## F

**Free Software**
Software that grants users four essential freedoms: to run the program, to study and modify the source code, to redistribute copies, and to distribute modified versions. Defined by the Free Software Foundation. Functionally overlaps with open source software in most policy contexts; the FSFE prefers this term because "free" emphasises freedom rather than cost or access. Relevant to: Open Source in Government.

**Free Software Foundation Europe (FSFE)**
A European non-profit organisation advocating for Free Software in Europe, including the "Public Money? Public Code!" campaign. Relevant to: Open Source in Government, Digital Sovereignty.

---

## G

**GDPR (General Data Protection Regulation)**
EU Regulation 2016/679, establishing rights for individuals regarding their personal data and obligations for organisations that process it. The GDPR's rights architecture — including rights of access, rectification, erasure, portability, and objection — is widely considered the most comprehensive privacy rights framework globally. Relevant to: Smart Cities & Privacy, Algorithmic Accountability, Children & Technology.

---

## H

**High-Risk AI System**
Under the EU AI Act, an AI system classified as high-risk because it poses significant risk to health, safety, or fundamental rights. High-risk systems include those used in critical infrastructure, education, employment, essential services, law enforcement, migration, and the administration of justice. High-risk systems are subject to the Act's most stringent obligations. Relevant to: AI Adoption, Algorithmic Accountability.

**Human-in-the-Loop**
A design requirement that a human being reviews and approves automated decisions before they take effect. The term is sometimes used to describe systems where a human technically can intervene but in practice rarely does due to volume, time pressure, or interface design — these are not genuinely human-in-the-loop systems. Policy language should distinguish between meaningful human oversight and nominal compliance. Relevant to: AI Adoption, Algorithmic Accountability.

---

## I

**Interoperability**
The ability of different systems, products, or organisations to work together by exchanging and using information. Technical interoperability (data formats, APIs) is distinct from organisational interoperability (processes and governance) and semantic interoperability (shared meaning). In digital policy, interoperability by design means systems are built to interoperate from the outset, rather than providing interoperability on request. Relevant to: Right to Repair, Open Source in Government, Digital Sovereignty.

---

## L

**Lock-in**
A state of dependency in which switching from one vendor, system, or platform to an alternative is prohibitively costly — not because alternatives don't exist but because proprietary data formats, contracts, or integrations make switching impractical. Both technical lock-in (format/API dependency) and contractual lock-in (penalties and restrictions) are relevant in digital policy. Relevant to: Open Source in Government, Digital Sovereignty, Right to Repair.

---

## O

**Once-Only Principle**
The principle that citizens and businesses should provide data to government only once, and that government agencies must reuse that data rather than requiring it to be provided again. Enshrined in the EU Single Digital Gateway Regulation (2018/1724) and the Estonian Public Information Act (2007). Relevant to: Digital Public Infrastructure, Digital Sovereignty.

**Open Source Initiative (OSI)**
The organisation that maintains the Open Source Definition and the authoritative list of approved open source licences. A licence is considered open source if it meets the OSI's ten criteria, which include free redistribution, access to source code, and no discrimination against persons or fields of endeavour.

**Open Source Programme Office (OSPO)**
An organisational unit responsible for managing an institution's open source activity, including licence compliance, contribution strategy, software cataloguing, policy guidance, and community engagement. OSPOs originated in large technology companies and have since spread to universities, government agencies, and the European Commission. Relevant to: Open Source in Government.

**Open Standard**
A standard that is publicly available, developed through an open process with broad participation, and implementable without royalty or restriction. Open standards are a precondition for interoperability and a key component of digital sovereignty. Relevant to: Digital Sovereignty, Open Source in Government, Right to Repair.

## P

**Parts Pairing**
A software-enabled restriction in which a device refuses to function correctly with a replacement component unless that component has been cryptographically paired to the device by the manufacturer. Parts pairing prevents independent repair even when the physical part is compatible and is a key target of right to repair legislation. Relevant to: Right to Repair.

**Power Usage Effectiveness (PUE)**
A metric for data center energy efficiency: total facility energy divided by IT equipment energy. A PUE of 1.0 is theoretically perfect; industry average is approximately 1.5; leading facilities achieve below 1.2. Required reporting metric in several jurisdictions' data center regulations. Relevant to: Data Centers.

**publiccode.yml**
A YAML metadata file placed in a software repository that describes the software in a machine-readable way, enabling automated discovery and cataloguing. Mandatory for public software in Italy; adopted as the reference standard for the EU Open Source Solutions Catalogue (2025). Relevant to: Open Source in Government.

---

## R

**Repairability Index / Score**
A numerical score indicating how easy a product is to repair, based on criteria such as availability of spare parts, access to repair documentation, disassembly difficulty, and software unlocking. France introduced a mandatory repairability index (indice de réparabilité) in 2021 under the AGEC law. Relevant to: Right to Repair.

**Right to Explanation**
The right of an individual to receive a meaningful explanation of the reasoning behind an automated decision that affects them. GDPR Article 22 establishes a limited right not to be subject to solely automated decisions with significant effects, and Article 13-15 require disclosure of the "logic involved." The right to explanation in practice is significantly weaker than in principle due to definitional gaps and enforcement failure. Relevant to: Algorithmic Accountability, AI Adoption.

---

## S

**Security Through Obscurity**
The practice of relying on secrecy of design or implementation as a primary security mechanism. Security experts broadly consider this insufficient: systems should be designed to be secure even if their internal logic becomes known. Relevant to: Open Source in Government.

**Sovereign Tech Fund / Sovereign Tech Agency**
Germany's government-funded body (formally established as an agency in 2024) that invests in the maintenance and security of open source components critical to digital infrastructure. By late 2024 it had invested approximately €23.5 million in over 60 projects. The model has influenced discussions about an EU-level equivalent fund. Relevant to: Open Source in Government.

## W

**WCAG (Web Content Accessibility Guidelines)**
Technical guidelines published by the W3C Web Accessibility Initiative (WAI) defining how to make web content accessible to people with disabilities. WCAG 2.2, published October 2023, is the current version. WCAG 2.1 Level AA is the legal standard in the EU Web Accessibility Directive and many national frameworks. Relevant to: Digital Accessibility.

**Water Usage Effectiveness (WUE)**
A metric for data center water efficiency: the volume of water used for cooling divided by the energy consumed by IT equipment. Data centers use significant water for evaporative cooling; WUE disclosure helps communities understand the local water impact of facilities. Relevant to: Data Centers.

**Web Sustainability Guidelines (WSG)**
A W3C draft document (Note, not a Standard) providing guidance on designing and operating websites and digital services to minimise environmental impact. Covers user experience design, web development, hosting infrastructure, and business strategy. 92 guidelines with 254 success criteria as of the 2025 draft. Relevant to: Web Sustainability.
