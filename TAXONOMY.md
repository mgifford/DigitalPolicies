# ODP Taxonomy — Plain-Language Domain Names

> How we name things determines who finds them and who feels invited in.
> This document maps the technical policy domains to public-facing names designed for SEO, advocacy, and broad community engagement.

---

## The Problem with Policy Names

"Algorithmic accountability" is accurate. "Interoperability mandates" is precise. "Digital sovereignty frameworks" is correct.

None of them will be searched by a farmer who can't fix their tractor, a carer who can't get their parent's medical device repaired, a parent worried about what their child's school app is collecting, or a community organiser asking why a data center just arrived in their town and is drawing down the aquifer.

OCRP's genius was tying climate policy to language people already care about: "urban forests," not "vegetative infrastructure policy." We need to do the same.

---

## Proposed Public-Facing Themes

Each theme below maps to one or more of our technical policy domains. The theme name is designed to be:
- Searchable by someone who doesn't know the policy terminology
- Tied to a lived experience or concern
- Emotionally legible — it says what's at stake

---

### 🔌 "Who Pays for AI's Power Bill?"
**Maps to:** Data Centers & Environmental Sustainability
**What it covers:** Energy consumption of AI and cloud computing; community impacts of data center siting; water use; fossil fuel generation; renewable energy claims; community benefit agreements

**Why this framing:** The AI boom has made data center energy visible. "Why is my electricity bill going up?" and "Why does my town have a water shortage since that facility opened?" are real questions being asked. This frames it as a cost question — who benefits, who pays.

**Search terms it captures:** data center energy, AI power consumption, data center water use, cloud computing electricity, AI environmental impact, data center community impact

**Related terms to build content around:**
- AI's water footprint
- Cloud computing carbon
- Data center in my backyard
- Who pays for AI's electricity

---

### 🤖 "AI That Works For You, Not On You"
**Maps to:** AI Adoption & Governance; Algorithmic Accountability
**What it covers:** AI in public services (benefits, hiring, criminal justice, healthcare); transparency requirements; the right to a human review; algorithmic discrimination; automated decision-making

**Why this framing:** People experience AI as something that happens *to* them — their loan application denied by an algorithm, their job application filtered out, their benefits cut. The framing "works for you, not on you" names the power dynamic and implies that a different relationship is possible.

**Search terms it captures:** AI bias, algorithmic discrimination, AI in hiring, automated decision making, AI in government, AI accountability, AI transparency, algorithmic accountability, AI welfare policy, AI and benefits

**Related terms:**
- Can a computer decide if I get benefits?
- AI denied my loan
- Automated hiring discrimination
- AI in the justice system
- Right to a human review

### 💧 "Clean Tech Shouldn't Cost Clean Water"
**Maps to:** Data Centers; Web Sustainability; E-Waste
**What it covers:** Data center water consumption; e-waste export and its effects on communities in the Global South; the environmental justice dimensions of digital infrastructure

**Why this framing:** This is an environmental justice framing that connects digital technology to natural resource access — a concern that crosses many political boundaries. It also connects the digital economy to the climate justice movement.

**Search terms it captures:** data center water use, e-waste Africa, electronics waste dumping, AI environmental impact, digital carbon footprint, tech and environment, electronics recycling, digital colonialism

### 🔒 "Your City Is Watching You"
**Maps to:** Smart Cities & Privacy; Children & Technology; Freedom of Expression
**What it covers:** Surveillance cameras and facial recognition in public spaces; smart city sensor data; location tracking; predictive policing; children's data in schools and apps

**Why this framing:** "Smart city" is a techno-optimist term that obscures the surveillance dimension. "Your city is watching you" is honest about the power relationship and invites people to ask: should it be? On whose terms?

**Search terms it captures:** facial recognition public spaces, smart city surveillance, CCTV AI, predictive policing, school surveillance, children's privacy apps, location tracking government, smart city privacy

**Related terms:**
- Does my city use facial recognition?
- Smart city and privacy
- School apps and children's data
- Location tracking in public
- Biometric surveillance ban

### 📱 "You Own It. You Should Be Able to Fix It."
**Maps to:** Right to Repair & Interoperability; E-Waste
**What it covers:** Software locks that prevent repair; parts pairing; manufacturer monopolies on repair; e-waste; device longevity; agricultural equipment repair; medical device repair; phone and laptop repair

**Why this framing:** This is already the language of the right to repair movement — populist, concrete, and cross-partisan. Farmers can't fix their tractors. Hospitals can't service their own ventilators. You can't replace a phone battery without voiding your warranty. The framing is already proven.

**Search terms it captures:** right to repair, phone repair rights, fix your own stuff, software lock repair, John Deere repair, farm equipment repair software, e-waste, planned obsolescence, battery replacement right, laptop repair

**Related terms:**
- Why can't I fix my own phone?
- John Deere software lock
- Medical device right to repair
- Manufacturer repair monopoly
- Battery replacement law

### 🌐 "Public Money, Public Code"
**Maps to:** Open Source in Government; Digital Sovereignty; Right to Repair
**What it covers:** Open source mandates for government software; vendor lock-in in government IT; digital sovereignty; public money spent on software that communities can't inspect, audit, or maintain

**Why this framing:** "Public Money, Public Code" is already the campaign name used by FSFE and has strong recognition in the open source community. It's worth building on this existing movement rather than inventing new language.

**Search terms it captures:** open source government, public money public code, government software vendor lock-in, FOSS government mandate, open source public sector, digital sovereignty, government IT transparency

**Related terms:**
- Should governments use open source?
- Government software we can't see
- Open source for public services
- Vendor lock-in government
- Digital sovereignty

### ♿ "Digital Services That Work for Everyone"
**Maps to:** Digital Accessibility & Inclusion
**What it covers:** WCAG compliance; screen reader compatibility; accessible procurement; accessibility of AI interfaces; plain language requirements; disability rights in digital services

**Why this framing:** "Accessibility" as a word has become jargon. "Digital services that work for everyone" is plainly descriptive and implies inclusion rather than legal compliance. It invites people who aren't disability advocates to care.

**Search terms it captures:** web accessibility, disability and technology, screen reader, WCAG, accessible website, ADA compliance website, digital inclusion, accessible government services, assistive technology

**Related terms:**
- Why doesn't this website work with my screen reader?
- Government websites accessibility
- Digital accessibility law
- WCAG compliance
- Accessible AI

### 🧒 "Technology That's Safe for Kids"
**Maps to:** Children & Technology
**What it covers:** Data collection from children; algorithmic amplification and mental health; age-appropriate design; targeted advertising to minors; school surveillance apps; parental rights and children's autonomy in digital spaces

**Why this framing:** "Children's online safety" is already highly searched. The framing "safe for kids" is immediate and parental. It should be used carefully, however — the policy space is contested between parental control advocates and children's rights advocates, and model language must hold both.

**Search terms it captures:** children online safety, kids privacy apps, screen time policy, social media children, school surveillance apps, COPPA, children's data, age-appropriate design, kids mental health social media

**Related terms:**
- What does my child's app collect?
- Social media and teen mental health
- School apps privacy
- Age verification children
- Children's online rights

---

### 🗣️ "Who Decides What You Can Say Online?"
**Maps to:** Freedom of Expression & Content Governance
**What it covers:** Platform content moderation; automated content removal; appeal rights; government pressure on platforms; algorithmic amplification of harmful content; DSA-style transparency requirements

**Why this framing:** This is the most contested domain in digital policy, and the framing needs to hold the tension rather than resolve it. "Who decides?" is a governance question — it doesn't presuppose an answer, which is appropriate given genuine disagreement in this space.

**Search terms it captures:** content moderation, free speech online, platform censorship, social media algorithm, who decides online content, digital censorship, content removal appeal, online speech rights

---

### 🌱 "A Greener Web"
**Maps to:** Web Sustainability
**What it covers:** Website carbon footprint; green web hosting; efficient code; digital carbon reporting; sustainable web design; W3C Web Sustainability Guidelines

**Why this framing:** The Sustainable Web Design movement has already established "greener web" as common shorthand. This domain is pre-legislative — model language is aspirational — but the public framing already has community.

**Search terms it captures:** website carbon footprint, green web hosting, sustainable web design, digital carbon, website emissions, W3C sustainability guidelines, green internet

## Cross-Cutting Themes

Several themes cut across multiple domains and should be used as navigation entry points on any public website:

**"Who is this for?"** → Accessibility, Children, Digital Inclusion
**"Who's watching?"** → Smart Cities, Surveillance, Children
**"Who controls it?"** → Digital Sovereignty, Open Source, Interoperability
**"Who pays?"** → Data Centers, E-Waste, Environmental Justice
**"Can I fix it?"** → Right to Repair, Device Longevity, E-Waste
**"Is it fair?"** → AI Accountability, Algorithmic Bias, Content Moderation
**"Is it safe?"** → Children, Encryption, Surveillance

## SEO and Discovery Strategy

For each policy domain, the public site should have:

1. **A plain-language landing page** using the theme name (e.g., "You Own It. You Should Be Able to Fix It.") with the technical domain name as subtitle
2. **A news/blog feed** tracking current legislative developments — this is where SEO and recency value live
3. **Real stories** — one paragraph human examples at the top of each domain (the farmer, the parent, the wheelchair user, the small repair shop owner)
4. **Clear calls to action** — "Use this language in your submission," "Share this with your councillor," "Download the model ordinance"
5. **Social-proof examples** — "This language was adopted in [City/State/Country]" as early wins accumulate

The GitHub repo is the authoritative source. The public site is the discovery layer. These serve different audiences and should be designed differently.

## Audience Mapping

| Audience | Entry point | What they need |
|---|---|---|
| Community organiser | Plain-language theme page | Human story, clear ask, shareable summary |
| Local government councillor | Plain-language theme + real examples | "What other cities have done," model ordinance text |
| Government procurement officer | Standards section of policy model | Specific language for RFPs, ACR/VPAT requirements |
| Legal practitioner | Full model with rationale | Precedents, citations, model clauses |
| Journalist | Human story + policy context | What's happening, who's affected, who's resisting |
| Researcher | Full model + citations + gap sections | Evidence quality, what's missing, where to contribute |
| Developer / open source contributor | GitHub repo | Markdown, PRs, issues |
| Policy advocate at UN / intergovernmental | Full models + adoption tracker | International precedents, model treaty language |
