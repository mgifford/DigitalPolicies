# Adoptions Tracker

This file documents known adoptions or near-adoptions of model language from Open Digital Policies — cases where a jurisdiction has enacted, proposed, or is actively considering language that matches or is substantially similar to the models in this repository.

**This tracker is almost certainly incomplete.** If you know of an adoption not listed here, please open a Pull Request or Issue with the label `adoption`.

---

## How to Read This Tracker

**Status definitions:**

| Status | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Enacted` | Legally in force |
| `Proposed` | Formally introduced as legislation or policy but not yet adopted |
| `In Development` | Known to be in active drafting; not yet formally introduced |
| `Influenced` | Policy substantially similar to model language, enacted independently (possibly predating ODP) |

**Match types:**

| Match | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `Direct` | Language from this repository was used or cited |
| `Parallel` | Similar language developed independently — useful as validation and example |

## Notes on Methodology

This tracker documents parallel developments as well as direct adoptions because:

1. **Parallel development validates model language** — when multiple jurisdictions independently arrive at similar provisions, it strengthens the case that the approach is sound and implementable.
2. **These jurisdictions are sources, not adopters** — most entries in this tracker predate ODP and are cited as real-world examples in the policy models. As ODP matures and is used by advocates, direct adoptions may be added.
3. **Attribution is complex** — policy is developed through many hands and influences. We document similarity, not causation.
